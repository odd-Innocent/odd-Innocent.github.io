# AEC Weekly Insight | 2026-04-26 ~ 2026-05-03

### Executive Summary

The Architecture, Engineering, and Construction (AEC) industry is currently undergoing a fundamental paradigm shift from a focus on technical data exchange schemas to the implementation of executable lifecycle governance. Recent research suggests that while vendor-neutral standards like Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) are technically capable of supporting Facility Management (FM) integration, the primary bottleneck has moved to the "owner-side" runtime—specifically, the ability to compile, validate, and execute complex requirements across the project lifecycle. This week’s developments highlight a move toward "construction data spaces" that emphasize trusted sharing and authority semantics over simple file interoperability.

### Critical Developments

**The Transition from Data Exchange to Runtime Governance**
A critical synthesis of recent interoperability reviews (2024, 2025) and open-standard prototypes indicates that the industry's historical obsession with "semantic exchange" may be reaching a point of diminishing returns. Research focusing on AECO interoperability (2024) and BIM–FM reviews (2025) suggests that vendor-neutral commercialization is no longer primarily hindered by isolated schema support or the lack of data formats. Instead, the blockage lies in the lack of a portable lifecycle-governance runtime. This runtime is required to bridge the gap between construction-phase revision control and FM-phase handover acceptance. The implication for the industry is a necessary pivot: rather than investing in more retrieval-centric benchmarks or schema-centric demonstrations, resources should be directed toward "lifecycle contract runtimes" that manage the governance of data throughout its evolution.

**Distributed Data Spaces and the "Authority" Semantics**
The emergence of "construction data spaces," as detailed in the DigiChecks (2024) framework, introduces a vital distinction in how AEC stakeholders manage compliance. The research suggests that while distributed, trusted data-sharing can significantly reduce friction in permit and compliance workflows, it does not inherently solve the problem of data "supersession"—the question of which data version holds legal or operational authority at a given moment. This development points toward a future where AEC systems must combine data-space infrastructure with explicit semantics for authority and release. This moves the conversation beyond mere interoperability toward a framework of "digital trust" where the lineage and authorization of every data point are verifiable.

**The Scalability Crisis in Owner Requirements**
Evidence from longitudinal studies of owner-side BIM automation (Tsay et al., 2023) reveals a massive scale problem that current standards are struggling to address. Documentation of thousands of distinct owner requirements across diverse portfolios suggests that current methods of structuring Information Delivery Specifications (IDS) or Level of Information Need (LOIN) are insufficient for commercial-scale automation. The bottleneck has reframed from "how do we format the data" to "how do we execute the governance of these thousands of requirements." This finding supports the need for a public, owner-side compiler or runtime layer that can interpret and validate these requirements in real-time, making IFC-BIM handover a functional reality rather than a manual checking exercise.

### Research Spotlight

**Owner-Intent Semantics and the Handover Bottleneck**
A significant area of focus this week involves the cross-analysis of Ashworth et al. (2018) with more recent ethnographic studies of BIM-FM handover (2022). For years, the industry has operated under the assumption that better guidance on Employer’s Information Requirements (EIR) would solve the handover gap. However, ethnographic evidence suggests that even with improved literacy in OIR (Organizational Information Requirements) and AIR (Asset Information Requirements), the practical execution of these intents remains fractured. 

The research methodology utilized in these studies combines technical prototyping with ethnographic observation, revealing that the "handover gap" is as much a behavioral and contractual issue as a technical one. The key finding is that future standardization work—particularly within the IFC framework—must prioritize "executable owner-intent semantics." This means moving beyond static document templates to digital protocols that can be programmatically verified against the "as-built" model. The practical potential is a system where the "Owner's Intent" acts as a compiled set of rules that the construction data must satisfy before the project can be contractually considered complete.

### Emerging Directions

**From Schema Mapping to Ontology Integration**
Building on the 2022 open IFC FM prototypes and the 2024 reviews of IFC-ontology integration, a clear direction is emerging toward the use of semantic web technologies to manage the complexity of FM requirements. Research suggests that the challenge is no longer about mapping one schema to another, but about creating a "lifecycle governance layer" that can span multiple proprietary and open formats. This direction favors the development of vendor-neutral commercial tools that focus on the "acceptance" phase of the lifecycle—compiling owner requirements into a runtime that can judge the fitness of incoming data from various AEC participants.

**Trusted Data Space Infrastructure**
Following the DigiChecks (2024) model, there is increasing momentum toward "Construction Data Spaces." Unlike traditional Common Data Environments (CDEs), which often act as centralized silos, these data spaces focus on decentralized, sovereign data sharing. The research direction is shifting toward how these spaces can handle "supersession" semantics—ensuring that all stakeholders are working off the most authoritative version of a document while maintaining a tamper-proof audit trail of changes and approvals.

### Implications

**For AEC Decision-Makers**
1. **Shift Investment to Acceptance Tools:** Decision-makers should move away from purchasing tools that merely "view" or "edit" BIM data and toward platforms that can "compile" and "validate" data against specific owner requirements. The value is no longer in the data itself, but in the ability to verify its compliance with the contract.
2. **Prioritize Data Governance over Formats:** When selecting software or establishing project protocols, focus on the "authority" and "release" semantics. Ensure your systems can clearly identify which stakeholder has the authority to release specific data and how that release triggers subsequent compliance checks.
3. **Develop Internal "Requirement Libraries":** Given the thousands of requirements documented in recent research (Tsay et al., 2023), organizations should begin digitizing their OIRs and AIRs into machine-readable formats that can eventually be fed into automated validation runtimes.

**For Academic Researchers**
1. **Focus on Executable Semantics:** There is a significant gap in the literature regarding how to turn "human-readable" intent into "machine-executable" validation rules. Research should target the development of compilers for owner requirements.
2. **Explore Data Space Governance:** Future work should investigate how "authority" and "supersession" can be managed in decentralized data spaces without reverting to centralized, proprietary control.
3. **Lifecycle Runtime Prototypes:** Rather than creating more schema-mapping benchmarks, researchers should focus on prototyping "lifecycle runtimes" that can maintain the integrity of a project's digital twin from the first design intent to the final FM handover.
